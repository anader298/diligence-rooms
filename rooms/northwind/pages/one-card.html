The contract in one sentence

Avery Freight Analytics builds the network-planning analysis that decides where freight should flow across 31 warehouses and the nine terminals that came with Tailspin, and proves each decision in cost per shipment before the trucks move.

The line

"We build the model that says which lanes and buildings earn their place, and we take the change through operations without breaking service. What we have not done is a network this size. What we have done is the analysis and the rollout, end to end, on a smaller one."

Four numbers to hold

  • 31 warehouses and 9 Tailspin terminals. Say "about forty sites" only if they do first.
  • 2,400 staff, their figure, from the 2025 careers page.
  • Revenue: never say a number. They say "more than half a billion" in one release. Not public beyond that.
  • Tailspin closed in Q3 2025. Integration is the reason this contract exists.

Three questions, in order

  1. "Of the nine Tailspin terminals, how many do you expect to keep running as terminals a year from now, and who makes that call?"
  2. "Your 2025 letter says cost per shipment is the number. Is that measured the same way across Northwind and Tailspin yet?"
  3. "If we started in April, what would you want off Theo's desk by July?"

Three terms in plain words

  • Lane: a regular origin-to-destination route. Northwind prices and plans by lane.
  • Cross-dock: freight comes in one door and leaves by another the same day, never stored.
  • Density: how full the trucks and buildings run. Their word for what the integration is supposed to fix.

Logistics

Video call, 45 minutes, three people. Camera on, notes closed. This card is read before, not open during.
