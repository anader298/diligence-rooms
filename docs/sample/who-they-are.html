    The short version

    • A Midwest regional carrier: 31 warehouses, 640 tractors, about 2,400 staff, majority-owned by a private equity fund since 2023.
    • Bought Tailspin Freight in 2025 and now has nine extra terminals, three in cities where it already runs one.
    • The ABC contract exists to decide which of those 40 sites earn their place, and to prove it in cost per shipment.

    What drives Northwind

    Four pulls on every decision they make this year, each with the evidence behind it.

    Financial

    Margin from the Tailspin overlap before the fund sells.

    Contoso bought in 2023; Fund III's last three exits came four to five years after the deal. A term loan from First Lakes Bank carries a leverage test. Inference Source: UCC filing

    Social

    A family carrier that still wants to look like one.

    Founded 1987; the family kept a board seat. Northwind sponsors the Chicago food bank's golf outing and calls itself “a Midwest employer of 2,400”. Verified Source: release

    Commercial

    Density, service, cost per shipment, in that order.

    Nine Tailspin terminals sit in three cities where Northwind already runs one. The 2025 letter promises “every building earns its place” without breaking the service promise. Verified Source: 2025 letter

    Technical

    One TMS by June, and a network model their own people run.

    The Tailspin terminals move to Northwind's TMS in June 2026. The Director of Network Planning seat, posted in January, is written around “owning the network model”. Verified Source: job post

    Sites by year

    Warehouses grew steadily before the fund invested and have held at 31 since 2024. The jump in 2025 is Tailspin. Verified Source: locations page

    Who owns it

    The fund holds the majority through a holding company; the family kept a minority and one board seat; a regional bank lends against everything. Verified Source: UCC filing Source: release
